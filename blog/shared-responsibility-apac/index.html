Banks in the Philippines

Part 1

The Philippines: The End of "Blame the Customer"

For years, the standard response to many bank scam cases in the Philippines was relatively straightforward: if the customer approved the transaction, the liability largely sat with them.

As of May 2026, that approach is rapidly changing.

With the implementation of the Anti-Financial Account Scamming Act (AFASA), Republic Act No. 12010, the Philippines is moving toward a significantly stronger Shared Responsibility framework across banks, e-wallets, digital platforms, and financial ecosystems.

For financial institutions, the conversation is shifting from "best efforts" toward demonstrating a far higher operational standard of diligence, fraud prevention, and customer protection.

Restitution and the New Duty of Care

Under AFASA, the burden around fraud accountability is evolving. Financial institutions may face significantly greater exposure where losses are linked to weaknesses in fraud controls, authentication processes, or broader operational safeguards.

One major area of focus is authentication.

The Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) has accelerated industry expectations around the migration away from traditional SMS OTPs for higher-risk transactions in favor of more phishing-resistant authentication methods.

In practice, this increasingly includes:

  • Server-side biometrics
  • Passkeys and FIDO-based authentication
  • Device intelligence
  • Real-time behavioral monitoring

Institutions continuing to rely heavily on legacy authentication methods may face growing scrutiny around whether they exercised an adequate "Duty of Care" under the evolving framework.

The End of Siloed Fraud Response

Shared responsibility is no longer only a policy discussion. It is increasingly becoming an operational expectation.

AFASA introduces stronger mechanisms for coordination, reporting, and fraud response between institutions in an effort to disrupt mule-account activity before funds disappear across the ecosystem.

This includes:

  • Faster interbank coordination
  • Greater fraud intelligence sharing
  • Enhanced monitoring of suspicious accounts
  • Stronger scrutiny around onboarding controls and mule detection

Importantly, the conversation is also expanding toward accountability across the transaction chain, including institutions holding receiving accounts or "mule" accounts.

The Technical Hardening of the Banking Stack

The Philippines is also accelerating broader fraud-management expectations across the industry.

Institutions are increasingly expected to strengthen:

  • Real-time fraud monitoring
  • Behavioral anomaly detection
  • Remote access and device compromise detection
  • Authentication resilience against phishing and social engineering

The direction is becoming increasingly clear: Fraud prevention is evolving from reactive monitoring into continuous behavioral and ecosystem-wide risk analysis.

The Vendor Play: Why Partnerships Matter

Partnerships

In a Shared Responsibility environment, institutions are no longer only selling financial services. They are increasingly expected to demonstrate operational trust and regulatory defensibility.

This creates a growing role for strategic partners across:

  • Customer communication and trust narratives
  • Fraud awareness and onboarding journeys
  • Cross-industry collaboration initiatives
  • Operational readiness and ecosystem coordination
  • Documentation and audit support

As regulators place greater emphasis on coordinated fraud prevention, partnerships between banks, telcos, platforms, and advisory ecosystems are becoming increasingly important.

The Bottom Line

Across APAC, the question is no longer simply:

"How do we stop scams?"

It is increasingly becoming:

"How is accountability shared when prevention fails?"

The Philippines is emerging as one of the clearest examples of how fraud prevention, authentication standards, operational resilience, and ecosystem accountability are converging into a new financial services reality.
